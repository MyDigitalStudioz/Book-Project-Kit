Generalized from the Book 1 launch email Jon sent Lois (2026-11-05, his wording and structure). For the moment a book goes live on Lulu. Placeholders in <angle brackets>.

---

Subject: <Book Title> is live! 🐱

Hi <Name>,

Great news — your book is officially published and up for sale on Lulu! Both versions are live:

Hardcover ($<price>):
<Lulu hardcover product link>

Paperback ($<price>):
<Lulu paperback product link>

You can order copies right from those links whenever you're ready, just like a regular store. So you know how the prices break down: almost all of it is just the cost of printing. The hardcover costs $<print cost> to print and sells for $<price>; the paperback costs $<print cost> to print and sells for $<price>. The little difference comes back to us as the author share — $<share> on a hardcover, $<share> on a paperback.

And here's the book online to flip through, page by page — this is exactly what's in the printed copies:
<flipbook link>

Once you have a printed copy in your hands, that's our final check of everything. And don't worry — if you spot anything you'd like changed, we can still make updates or revisions anytime. Nothing is set in stone.

Love,
<Your name>

---

## Why it's built this way (keep these beats)
- **Both formats with prices + direct links** — the recipient orders their own copies, no middleman.
- **The honest cost breakdown** — print cost vs price vs author share, in plain words. It preempts every "where does the money go" question.
- **The flipbook link** framed as "exactly what's in the printed copies" — the digital twin of the physical book.
- **Proof-in-hand = final check**, and **revisions stay possible** — reassurance, not a gate.
